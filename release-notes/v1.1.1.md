# v1.1.1 — Liber Loagaeth: structural study, with the layout of the two series on 25b–28b

Working paper (not peer reviewed), with its code, data and results. The paper is `paper/liber-loagaeth.pdf` in this archive.

**What changed.** Documentation update: adds a "How AI was used" section, tidies wording and removes files not needed to check the results. Results and data unchanged.

**Abstract.** *Liber Loagaeth* (British Library, Sloane MS 3189), the book of letter tables that John Dee and Edward Kelley received in 1583, has never been read, and this paper does not try to read it. It asks how the tables were made. From Joseph Peterson's cell-by-cell edition we build a coordinate-addressed corpus of 196,353 letters on 96 sides; the only other machine-readable grids turn out to copy the same edition, and 78 of 80 randomly chosen cells agree with the page images (2 doubtful, none wrong). We reproduce the one earlier computational study (Zubair 2026) and correct it: its letter total counts the whole web page, its lowest index of coincidence (0.044) belongs to an appendix of *Book of Soyga* tables, and its test of the Soyga generation rule used the wrong baseline; with each table's own letters as the baseline, no table departs from chance. We then ran ten tests that were written down and committed before scoring, each against 10,000 controls that keep the tables' own letter statistics, with Holm's correction. Six are positive: long strings recur across the book far beyond chance (6,679 distinct 8-letter strings against about 1,240 in controls), mostly on different sides; the word marks on leaves 2a–3b fall at letter-dependent places and cut long words (8.8 letters); and on 25b–28b Kelley's two interleaved letter series read as text-like sequences and differ in their letters. Four are negative: there is no pairing of a- and b-sides, the cell above does not predict a cell, and the 48 Calls are neither found in the rows more often than pseudo-words nor closer to the tables in letter order than Latin or English text. A second pre-registered study (version 1.1) finds the rule by which the two series of 25b–28b are laid: each is one colour of a chessboard, written one diagonal per line, so that its lines have exactly the row lengths of the partly filled tables of 29a–48b, and the slant of the letters in Peterson's marks gives the writing direction (7 of 9 marked series, and 2 of 3 held-out series; Holm p 0.0004 each); the two series of a side neither continue nor mirror each other. The evidence weighs toward tables built from a recurring stock of word-like strings, laid out row by row and varied gradually through the book, with the two-series tables written along diagonals, and against the Calls being drawn from the tables' rows. Nothing here is a decipherment or a translation.
